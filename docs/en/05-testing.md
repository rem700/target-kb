# Tests and problems

## How to try new things without breaking what works
A test is a small experiment: change one thing and see whether it got better. Change three things at once and you won't know which one worked.

**What to test in 2026:** video ideas and offers. Testing audiences is almost pointless — the robot picks them anyway.

!!! example "Anya's test"
    Anya wants to know what sells better: "cosy evening" or "gift for a colleague". She makes two videos of the same length and quality and runs them in a test campaign with the same budget.

### Rules of a good test
- Run tests in a **separate campaign**, so they don't disturb the main one.
- Wait **at least 3 days**. The first days are noise.
- Draw conclusions once a version has spent 1–2 "customer prices" (9–18 € for Anya) or got 50+ clicks.
- **Kill rule:** spent 2–3 customer prices with zero purchases — switch it off, no regrets.
- Move the winner into the main campaign **using the post ID**: that keeps its likes and comments, and they build trust.

## What not to do
Every big edit makes the robot learn from scratch. Like changing a teacher's syllabus every day.

- Don't change the audience, bid or objective in a working ad set.
- Don't switch ads off and on every day.
- Don't draw conclusions in the first 1–3 days.
- Don't change the budget sharply (doubling it at once).

## How to grow once it works
- **Add money gradually:** +15–20% budget every 2–3 days. A sharp jump disrupts learning. (practice)
- **Expand:** new countries or cities, new placements.
- **Make more different videos.** This is the main way to grow in 2026: every new video finds new people.

!!! warning "Remember"
    As the budget grows, customers usually get more expensive. The robot finds the "easiest" buyers first, then has to look further. Keep your limit in mind (9 € for Anya).

## First-aid kit: "the ads aren't working"
Find the symptom and check the likely causes.

**The ad isn't shown or isn't spending**
: the ad is still in review or was rejected; a payment problem; too low a cost limit; too narrow an audience.

**Impressions are very expensive**
: season (ads get pricier for everyone before holidays); too narrow an audience; a dull ad the system rates poorly.

**People see it but don't click** (fewer than 1 click per 100 impressions is a warning sign, but it depends on the niche)
: weak video opening; uninteresting offer; the ad looks too much like an ad.

**People click but don't buy**
: the site is slow or awkward on a phone; the site doesn't match what the ad promised; a complicated form; broken tracking.

**Leads but no customers**
: the leads are random. Use the "higher intent" form, add screening questions and reply fast — ideally within 5–15 minutes.

**It was good, now it's bad**
: most likely ad fatigue: frequency up, fewer clicks. You need new videos. Other options: season, competitors, platform changes (see "What's new") or broken tracking.

## Work rhythm
- **Daily, 5 minutes:** how much was spent, how many results, any rejected ads or payment issues.
- **Weekly:** switch off weak ads, add new videos, compare leads with real sales.
- **Monthly:** recalculate how much you can spend per customer and decide what to scale.
