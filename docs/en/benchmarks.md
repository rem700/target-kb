# Benchmarks

"My CTR is 1.2% — is that good?" Without reference points you can't tell. Below are averages from public 2026 reports.

!!! warning "Read with caution"
    Averages depend heavily on niche, country, campaign objective, season, and even on who collected the data. Most reports are based on US accounts. Use them only to tell whether you're "within reason" or "something's badly wrong". Your main reference is your unit economics and your own numbers from past months.

According to the same report, the average cost per click in Google Ads is more than double that of Meta.

## CPM by European country
According to Lebesgue (online shops, 2026):

- the most expensive CPM in Europe is the UK, around $11.8;
- the cheapest is Bulgaria, around $4.2;
- Western Europe is generally pricier than Eastern Europe.

According to Superads, the average CPM in Germany from October 2025 to September 2026 was around €14.8.

Hungary is closer to Central and Eastern European price levels: usually cheaper than Germany and the UK. There's little precise public data for Hungary, so your own first weeks are the best guide.

## Ads are getting pricier overall
It's not just the season. According to Meta's Q3 2026 results, the average price per ad rose 14% worldwide and 23% in the US and Canada, while impressions grew only 10%. The same money buys fewer impressions.

What follows:

- A rising CPM alone is no reason to panic. **Look at cost per customer:** if it stays within your limit, you're fine.
- Compare your numbers with the same period last year, not just last month.
- Review your unit economics quarterly: it may be time to raise prices or the average order value.

## How to use this
- **CPM 2–3 times above average** for your country: possibly too narrow an audience, a weak creative, or the season.
- **CTR well below 1%**: the creative or offer isn't grabbing people (see [Tests and problems](05-testing.md)).
- **High CTR, no conversions**: a problem on the site or with tracking.
- **Cost per lead below average but no sales**: check lead quality ([case 2](09-problem-cases.md)).

## Your own benchmarks
After 1–2 months you'll have your own averages. Keep a small table: month, CPM, CTR, cost per result. Compare yourself to yourself, not to other people's reports.
