# Creatives: why the picture decides

## The big news of recent years
Meta now has a system called **Andromeda** that chooses which ad to show to whom. It "watches" your video, reads the text, listens to the sound, and from that works out who the ad is for. (official/practice)

Which leads to a funny thing: **your ad sets up its own audience.**

!!! example "Anya's example"
    A video of a woman lighting a candle in the bath with a glass of wine — the robot will show it to people who love cosy evenings. A video called "the perfect gift for a colleague under 30 €" — to people looking for gifts. Same candle, two different audiences.

Hence the main rule: **you need different ads for different people**, not one ad "for everyone".

## What "different" means
If you change the caption colour or font in a video, to the robot it's almost the same ad. Change the meaning. At least one thing:

- **Why buy**: cosiness, a gift, eco-friendliness, status, "get it before the holidays".
- **Who's in the frame and who it's for**: a woman buying for herself, a man looking for a gift, a mum with a child.
- **Format**: a person talking to camera, the making process, before and after, a review, a photo set.
- **The first seconds**: how the video starts.
- **The offer**: a discount, a set, free wrapping.

## Formats
- **Vertical video, 15–30 seconds** — the main format right now. Always with subtitles: most people watch without sound.
- **Carousel** (several photos in a row) — for comparisons and selections: "5 winter scents".
- **Image** — a quick check of whether the offer grabs people.

!!! tip "Don't chase polish"
    A video shot on a phone that looks like a normal post often beats an expensive production. Glossy ads shout "I'm an ad!" and get scrolled past.

## How to build a video
1. **0–3 seconds: grab them.** Show the result, ask a question, or open with an unexpected shot. A logo at the start is a sure way to get scrolled past.
2. **3–15 seconds: explain.** What it is, who it's for, why buy now.
3. **Prove it.** A review, the making process, how it looks in real life.
4. **Invite.** One action in one sentence: "Order on the website".

!!! example "Hooks for Anya"
    - "This candle burns for 60 hours. I checked."
    - "A gift that won't end up in a cupboard."
    - A close-up: match, flash, flame — and only then the text.

## The text under the video
- The first line continues the hook. It's visible before "more"; few people read the rest.
- Short, and about the benefit for the person.
- You can give up to 5 versions of the text and headline — Meta will pick combinations itself.

## How many videos you need
- **At launch**: 3–6 different ideas.
- **After that**: 1–3 new ideas every week or two. Ads "burn out" faster now than they used to. (practice)
- **Signs of fatigue**: frequency rising, fewer and fewer clicks, customers getting more expensive for a couple of weeks.

## About AI
Meta can extend backgrounds, stretch an image to fit a format, turn a photo into a video, pick music and rewrite text. Handy, but there's a catch: everyone uses these tools, so ads start to look the same. Real footage and real people are your advantage. Always reread generated text.

## Where to find ideas
- **Meta Ad Library** — a library of all active ads. See what competitors are running. If an ad has been live for months, it's probably making money.
- **TikTok Creative Center** — trends and popular ads.
- **Customer questions and comments** — ready-made hooks. If five people asked "how long does it burn?", that's your video's first line.
