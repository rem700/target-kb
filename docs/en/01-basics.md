# Basics and numbers

## How an ad is built: three floors
Every Meta ad is like a nesting doll with three layers:

- **Campaign**: why we're doing this at all. For example, "I want sales".
- **Ad set**: who we show it to, where, and how much we spend.
- **Ad**: what people actually see. An image or video plus text.

!!! example "Anya's example"
    Campaign "Christmas candle sales" → ad set "Hungary, ages 22–55, 20 € a day" → three ads: a video of a candle being lit; a photo of the gift box; a customer review.

## Mini-glossary: 10 words you can't do without
**Optimization**
: what you ask the robot to find. Clicks? Leads? Purchases? It will look for exactly that.

**Learning**
: the first days after launch, while the robot works out who to show ads to. It needs roughly 50 of the target actions per week. Results jump around while it learns — that's normal.

**Cold, warm, hot**
: cold people have never heard of you, warm ones have visited your profile or site, hot ones almost bought.

**Retargeting**
: ads that follow people who already visited. The reason those sneakers "chase" you around the internet.

**Lookalike audience**
: "find me people similar to my customers". The robot does this anyway now, so you rarely need it separately.

**Placement**
: where the ad appears: feed, Reels, Stories, Threads.

**Frequency**
: how many times, on average, one person has seen your ad.

**Fatigue**
: when people have seen the same ad too often and stop reacting. Like a song played on the radio for the tenth time today.

**Creative**
: the image or video in the ad.

**Offer**
: the deal itself. "Handmade candle" is a product; "Candle + free gift wrapping until 20 December" is an offer.

## Numbers worth understanding
Don't be scared of the acronyms — each one hides a simple question.

| Number | Simple question | How it's calculated |
|---|---|---|
| **CPM** | How much does it cost to show the ad 1000 times? | spend / impressions × 1000 |
| **CTR** | What share of people clicked? | clicks / impressions × 100% |
| **CPC** | How much does one click cost? | spend / clicks |
| **CR** | What share of site visitors bought or left a request? | leads / clicks × 100% |
| **CPA / CPL** | How much does one customer / one lead cost? | spend / purchases (leads) |
| **ROAS** | How many euros of revenue did each ad euro bring? | revenue / spend |
| **Frequency** | How many times has a person seen the ad? | impressions / reach |

Two more for video:

- **Hook rate**: how many people watched the first 3 seconds. Answers "did the opening grab them?"
- **Hold rate**: how many of them kept watching. Answers "was it interesting?"

## The chain for finding problems
A person goes: saw it → clicked → visited the site → bought. Each step has its number: **CPM → CTR → CR → CPA**. If results are bad, find the step where people "drop off".

- **Impressions are expensive (high CPM)**: season (in December everyone advertises), too narrow an audience, or a boring ad.
- **They see it but don't click (low CTR)**: the ad doesn't grab attention or the offer isn't interesting.
- **They click but don't buy**: something's wrong on the site. It loads slowly, is awkward on a phone, the price scares people off, or the form is broken.
- **They leave requests but don't buy**: the leads are junk. For example, people tapped by accident.

## How much you can spend per customer
Work this out before launch, or you won't know whether the ads are doing well or badly.

!!! example "Doing the maths for Anya"
    A candle costs 30 €, making it costs 12 €, so Anya earns 18 € per candle. She's willing to spend half of that on ads: **one customer must cost no more than 9 €.** If ads bring customers at 6 € — great. At 15 € — Anya is losing money.

If you collect leads and sell later: max cost per lead = max cost per customer × the share of leads that turn into purchases.

## How much money you need to start
The robot needs about 50 actions a week to learn. Hence a quick estimate:

**daily budget ≥ cost of one result × 50 / 7**

For Anya: 9 € × 50 / 7 ≈ 64 € a day. Too much? Then ask the robot to look for a more frequent action, like "added to cart" instead of "purchased". There are more of those, so the robot learns faster.
