# Working with a contractor

Sooner or later you'll want to hand ads to a specialist. It can save time and money — or cost you both. How to choose, brief and stay in control.

## Who you're looking for
- **Ads specialist / media buyer** — sets up and runs campaigns.
- **Content creator / mobile videographer** — films the videos.
- **Agency** — all of the above, pricier, but less of your involvement.

For a small business the best combo is often: you (or a creator) make the videos, a specialist runs Ads Manager.

## How to choose
**Good signs**
: shows case studies with money numbers (cost per customer, revenue), not just "1M reach"; asks about your economics, margin, CRM; explains things simply; talks about risks.

**Warning signs**
: guarantees results ("I guarantee 10x ROAS"); asks for admin access to your business portfolio or your Facebook password; offers to set up the ad account "in their name"; can't explain what they'll do; promises "secret settings" and "beating the algorithm"; promises to get around moderation for banned topics.

## A brief for the specialist
Give them at the start:

- what you sell, average order value, margin, how much you'll pay per customer ([unit economics](templates.md));
- who your customers are and where they come from now;
- what you've tried in ads and with what results;
- monthly budget and test period;
- access to tracking, site, CRM (as needed);
- who replies to leads and how fast;
- limits: what you can't promise, which topics are sensitive.

## Access: right and safe
- The ad account, pixel, Page and Instagram must be **owned by your** business portfolio.
- The contractor gets access as a **partner** (via their business portfolio ID) or as a person, with rights only to the ad account and pixel they need.
- Portfolio admin rights — **never**.
- Your personal Facebook password — **never**.
- When the work ends — remove access immediately.

!!! warning "Why this matters"
    If the ad account belongs to the contractor, when you part ways you lose all history, the pixel, audiences and sometimes the Page. If a former contractor keeps access, it's a risk of hacking and unauthorized spend (see [case 5](09-problem-cases.md)).

## How to agree
- **Trial period**: 1–2 months with a clear goal (e.g. cost per customer no higher than X).
- **Reporting**: a short weekly report (a template is in [Templates](templates.md)), a monthly call.
- **Payment**: a fixed fee, a percentage of ad spend, or a mix. A percentage of spend motivates spending more — keep that in mind.
- **Ad spend** is paid from your card in your ad account, not through the contractor.
- In the contract: who owns the creatives and data, notice period for ending the work.

## How to keep control without knowing everything
- Look at the **cost per customer** by your own data (shop, CRM), not reach and clicks.
- Check Ads Manager yourself once a week: how much was spent, how many results, any rejected ads.
- Ask "why": why was this ad switched off, why was the budget raised. A good specialist answers calmly and specifically.
